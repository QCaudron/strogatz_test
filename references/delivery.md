# Delivery: live chat, spoken talks and slides

**Untested; low confidence.** No evaluation in this project exercised any rule in this file, and most of its sources were read through summaries. Use it when the user asks how to deliver material, not only how to write it, and say in the note that the advice is untested.

The principle is the same as in writing: empathy for a specific reader, who should know why each step is taken and reach the point fast. What changes is that a listener cannot reread, and that a live reader can be asked a question. The user's bar still wins where his habits conflict with it.

Sources: his podcast, *The Joy of Why* (16 episodes, read mostly through summaries; tier P), his talks, and the tactics he states in his 2014 *Notices* essay (tier S). Ledger rows: R1, R2, W2, J1, C3, E2 and H1, with the podcast findings behind them.

## Live chat (an agent explaining in conversation)

- **Answer in the first sentence**, then give the smallest case, then the reason. The user came for an answer, so the point-first rule (R1) holds here more strictly than in a lesson. Tier U.
- **Ask the naive question for them.** Where a newcomer would stall, name the question and answer it before they have to ask ("You might wonder why the timeout is 30 seconds. It is because..."). On his podcast he does this at the moment a newcomer would stall, in 13 of 16 episodes. Tier P.
- **One idea per turn.** Stop after the idea and its case, rather than covering every branch; offer the next step in one line. Tier I.
- **Invite a prediction when it is cheap.** A single question the user can try ("What would you expect if the input doubled?") lets them take the last step themselves. Text alone cannot force this; a conversation can. Never make the answer depend on their reply. Tier L (pretesting helps when the learner tries to answer), I for chat.
- **End without a recap.** Close on the implication or the next question. He ends 14 of 16 episodes without one. Tier P.

## Spoken talks

- **Pose the question early.** His openings end by posing the question the episode will chase. A short scene before it is allowed only if it is the question (R2). Tier P.
- **Signpost direction and stakes** at each turn: where we are going and why it matters. All 16 episodes have explicit transitions. Tier P.
- **Never speak an equation.** Use numbers and small cases instead; no equation is spoken in any episode read. Tier P.
- **Example, then name, then the honest limit.** When he explains alone, he shows the thing, names it, and says what the model leaves out. Tier P.
- **Admit what is unknown, once**, and attach any wonder to a consequence, not to the topic. Tier S, P.
- **Restate and check back** with a new image, if there is a second speaker or questions are taken. It is his most frequent move as host (16 of 16). Tier P.
- **End without a recap**, on an implication, a limit or an open question. Tier P.

## Slides

- **Put each picture beside the words it illustrates**, and do not number figures. He states both tactics for print, to avoid a textbook feel. Tier S.
- **Avoid symbols the audience cannot sound out**, and keep algebra to a minimum; recast it as a picture where you can. Tier S.
- **One step per slide, with a headline that states its claim**, not its topic, so a reader who sees only the headlines still gets the argument. Tier I (the point-first rule applied to slides).
- **No bullets for a sequence.** Steps that depend on each other need a picture or a sentence with "because" or "so"; a bulleted slide drops the connections (F1). Tier U, I.
- **Show the small case before the general slide**, as in writing (C1). Tier P, L.
